How to Choose an Ergonomic Office Chair

If you sit for hours every day, your chair is the most important thing on your desk. A good one won’t make work fun, but it can stop the end-of-day aches that come from slouching.

What to look for

  • Lumbar support: the lower back should be supported, ideally with adjustable height or depth.
  • Seat height and depth: your feet should rest flat and there should be a small gap between the seat edge and the back of your knees.
  • Adjustable armrests: your shoulders should stay relaxed while typing.
  • Breathable material: mesh stays cooler during long sessions; padded fabric feels softer.
  • Warranty: a longer warranty usually signals a sturdier build.

Who it’s for

Anyone working from home more than a few hours a day. If you’re between sizes or very tall, check the weight and height limits before ordering.

Prices and models change often, so check the latest reviews and details on Amazon before you buy.

What to Look for in Noise-Cancelling Headphones

Noise-cancelling headphones are useful for open offices, flights, commutes and noisy homes. Marketing makes them all sound the same, so here is what actually matters.

Key features

  • Cancelling strength: works best on steady sounds like engines and fans, and less on voices.
  • Comfort: soft pads and light weight matter if you wear them for hours.
  • Battery life: aim for a full workday or more on one charge.
  • Microphone quality: important if you take lots of calls.
  • Connection: multipoint Bluetooth lets you switch between laptop and phone.

Tip

Read the three-star reviews. They usually describe real comfort and connection issues that five-star reviews skip.

Setting Up a Home Office on a Budget

You don’t need a showroom desk to work well from home. Spend on the things your body touches, and save on the rest.

Spend first on

  • A supportive chair
  • A monitor or laptop stand so the screen sits at eye level
  • Good lighting, ideally a dimmable desk lamp

Save on

  • Desk decor and fancy accessories
  • Cable organizers, where cheap clips and ties work fine
  • Second-hand desks, which are often great value

Easy upgrades

A wireless mouse, a small plant and a tidy cable setup make the space feel calm and cost very little.

Are Smart Plugs Worth It?

A smart plug turns an ordinary lamp or appliance into something you can switch on and off from your phone or with a timer. They’re one of the cheapest ways to try a smart home.

Good uses

  • Lamps that turn on at sunset
  • Switching off coffee makers or heaters you forgot
  • Holiday lights on a schedule

Before you buy

  • Make sure they work with your phone and voice assistant.
  • Check they support your Wi-Fi band (many need 2.4 GHz).
  • Check the power rating before using one with high-wattage appliances.

Keep smart plugs away from devices that could be unsafe if they switch on unattended.
